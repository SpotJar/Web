8. App data on your device

Everything you put into SpotJar is stored in the app’s private storage on your device: spots with their name, address, coordinates and the link they came from, notes, categories (built-in and your own), photos, status such as “experienced”, “must-see” or favorite, trips with their day plans, packing lists and templates, and your settings.

  • No account, no cloud backend. We operate no server and never receive this data.
  • Sharing into SpotJar. When you share a place from a map app to SpotJar, the link is handed to the app through a shared folder on your device. It does not pass through any server of ours.
  • Trip from photos (iOS only). If you create a trip from photos of a travel plan, text recognition (Apple Vision) and the analysis of that text (Apple Foundation Models) run entirely on your device. The photos and their text are not uploaded. The camera is only used for this, and only after you allow it.
  • Export. If you export your data, the app creates a file and you decide where it goes. Photos are only included if you choose so.
  • Device backups. Like most apps, SpotJar’s data can be part of the backup your device makes to iCloud or Google, depending on your device settings. That backup is between you and Apple or Google.

You delete data by deleting it in the app, or all of it by uninstalling the app.

9. Map & location services

A spots app needs a map and needs to know where a place is. SpotJar uses the map services built for your platform. Each of these requests carries your IP address, as any internet request does. None of them includes your notes, your categories or your other spots.

Showing the map

  • iOS: Apple MapKit (Apple Inc. / Apple Distribution International Ltd.). To draw the map, your device loads map data from Apple for the area you look at.
  • Android: Google Maps SDK for Android (Google Ireland Ltd. / Google LLC). To draw the map, your device loads map data from Google for the area you look at; the SDK also sends technical usage data to Google under Google’s privacy policy.

Finding a place and its address

When you save a spot, search for one by name, type an address, or pick a place on the map, the app looks it up to fill in name, address, coordinates and type:

  • iOS: through Apple MapKit search. Apple receives the search text and, if known, the approximate area to search in (the spot’s or the trip region’s coordinates).
  • Android: through the Google Places API. Google receives the search text or place identifier, if known the area to search in (the spot’s or the trip region’s coordinates), and the app’s identifier and API key.

Short links. Links like maps.app.goo.gl/... contain no place until they are opened. To find out which place such a link stands for, the app asks the link’s server (Google) where it leads, on both iOS and Android. Google receives the link itself.

Your location

Access to your location is optional and only requested “while using the app” on Android, only your approximate location. SpotJar asks for it when you sort spots by distance or open the map of a trip. Your position is used on your device to calculate distances and to show you on the map. It is not stored in your spots and not sent to us; it is also not used as the search area for place lookups. You can withdraw the permission at any time in your device’s settings; everything else keeps working.

Opening a spot or a route in Maps

When you open a spot, or a trip or day as a route, SpotJar hands the coordinates, and where available the name or address, of those spots to Apple Maps or Google Maps as an app or on the web, whichever you choose. That happens only when you tap it, and from then on the privacy policy of Apple or Google applies.

10. Automatic photos from Wikimedia

When you save a spot from a link (for example from Google Maps or Apple Maps, or through the share menu), SpotJar looks for a freely licensed photo of that place on Wikipedia and Wikimedia Commons after saving it. No lookup happens for spots you create by hand, place on the map or import from a backup, or for spots you have already given a photo of your own.

For this, the app sends the spot’s coordinates (latitude and longitude) and, as with any internet request, your IP address and an identifier of the app (name and version) to the Wikimedia Foundation, Inc., 1 Montgomery Street, Suite 1600, San Francisco, CA 94104, USA. The spot’s name, your notes and any other content are not sent; matching a photo to the spot happens on your device. If a suitable photo is found, the app downloads it from the Wikimedia Foundation’s servers.

The photo and its author and licence information are stored only on your device. You can remove such a photo or replace it with your own at any time in the spot’s edit screen; deleting the spot deletes it as well. The Wikimedia Foundation processes this data under its own privacy policy. The photos belong to their authors and are published under free licences (public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA); author, licence and source are shown directly below the photo.

You can object to this lookup at any time (Art. 21 GDPR) by writing to the address in section 1.

11. Crash reports & diagnostics

To find and fix errors, the app sends crash and error reports to Sentry, a service of Functional Software, Inc., 45 Fremont Street, 8th Floor, San Francisco, CA 94105, USA. SpotJar’s Sentry project is set up in Sentry’s EU region.

A report contains:

  • the error and the technical stack trace;
  • the app’s recent technical log messages leading up to it (internal identifiers of spots or trips, not their names or notes); an error message can occasionally contain a technical detail such as a map link that could not be processed;
  • device model, operating system and version, app version, and whether it is a test build;
  • your IP address, as with any internet request.

The app is configured not to send personal information by default, no screenshots, and no performance tracing. Reports are not linked to you or to any account because there is none.

You can object to crash reporting at any time (Art. 21 GDPR) by writing to the address in section 1. See also Sentry’s privacy policy.

12. International data transfers

Some of the providers named above are based in the USA or may process data there: GitHub (website hosting), Apple (maps on iOS), Google (maps and place search on Android, short links), the Wikimedia Foundation (photos) and Functional Software / Sentry (crash reports). Where a provider is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, the transfer is based on the European Commission’s adequacy decision of 10 July 2023 (Art. 45 GDPR). Otherwise it is based on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR).

14. Data retention

  • App data stays on your device until you delete it in the app or uninstall the app.
  • Location is used while the app runs and not stored.
  • Crash reports are deleted by Sentry after the retention period of the project, TODO: e.g. 90 days; confirm in Sentry.
  • Website logs, map and photo requests are kept by GitHub, Apple, Google and the Wikimedia Foundation according to their own policies; we have no access to them.

15. Security measures

App data lives in the app’s private storage, which the operating system shields from other apps. The app talks to the services named above over encrypted HTTPS connections, and this website is only served over HTTPS. Because there is no account and no server of ours, there is no central database of users that could leak.

16. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to:

  • access the personal data we process about you (Art. 15);
  • have it corrected (Art. 16) or erased (Art. 17);
  • restrict its processing (Art. 18);
  • receive it in a portable format (Art. 20);
  • object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21);
  • withdraw a consent you gave, with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)); for location access, simply in your device’s settings.

Since your app data never reaches us, you exercise most of these rights directly in the app. For everything else, write to the address in section 1. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, in particular in the EU member state where you live or work.
